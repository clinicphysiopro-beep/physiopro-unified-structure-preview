Knee Pain.

Knee pain can arise in different settings. The useful next question is how it behaves during the activities you need to return to.

What people may notice

Symptoms need context.

Pain, swelling, stiffness, catching, a sense of giving way, or difficulty with stairs, squatting, running, or jumping can all be relevant. No individual symptom confirms a meniscal, ligament, cartilage, or other diagnosis.

Assessment

Start with the full picture.

A focused assessment may consider onset, swelling, trauma, mechanical symptoms, prior knee history, movement, strength, and the functional tasks that are currently limited. Referral decisions depend on the full picture.

Rehabilitation principles

Build capacity around the person and the demand.

Rehabilitation planning commonly links movement, strength, load tolerance, and a return to the person’s work or sport demands. The appropriate components and progression differ across presentations and whether operative care is involved.

Activity modification should retain useful movement where safe and tolerable, then progress according to response and functional goals—not a generic timetable.

When to seek urgent help

Do not wait on warning signs.

Seek prompt assessment after a significant injury with deformity, inability to bear weight, a locked knee, rapidly increasing swelling, fever/redness, or new numbness/weakness.

Common question

What this page can—and cannot—tell you.

Can knee pain tell me exactly which structure is injured? No. A diagnosis requires clinical evaluation and sometimes further medical assessment, not a webpage symptom checklist.
